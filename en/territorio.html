The reservoir

Made by people,now wild

Built in the 1980s as a reservoir on the river Ofanto, Lake Conza turned farmland into an ecosystem. The water drew the vegetation, the vegetation drew the insects, the insects drew the bees.

Today the lake is the centre of an ecological corridor linking the Lucanian Apennines to the Sele valley. Reed beds, willow groves and wet meadows grow along its shores, home to the dice snake, the osprey and the grey heron.

The lake also shapes the honey seasons: the body of water softens spring frosts, lengthens the summer blooms and favours a late harvest.

The soil

The clayyou can tastein the honey

The soils around Conza are mostly clay, remnants of a Miocene seabed lifted up when the Apennines formed. Clay holds moisture through the dry months, so plants flower for longer, and releases minerals to whatever puts down roots in it.

Sulla is the clearest example: it loves heavy soils and gives the minerals back as a faint grassy, saline note in the honey. That is why we speak of cru: as in a wine with a sense of place, the soil is an ingredient.

The ecosystem

Biodiversitymeans quality

Priscilla's bees don't face a monoculture, but a landscape. Every flowering plant adds a nuance; every species living here is a sign of balance.

01

Sulla

Hedysarum coronarium, an annual legume that flowers in May on clay soils. Purple-red flowers rich in nectar. It is the basis of our Cru delle Argille.

02

Black locust

Native to North America, naturalised in the damp valleys. It flowers for only 8–12 days but is very rich in nectar: it gives a clear honey that crystallises slowly.

03

Wild flowers

Clover, alfalfa, sulla, knapweed, borage, rockrose, thyme, oregano. The more species in flower, the more complex our Millefiori.

04

The birds of the Oasis

Osprey, grey heron, whiskered tern, kingfisher, little bittern. Where such sensitive species live, the environment is healthy: for bees too.

05

Lake wildlife

Pike, carp, tench, eel, crayfish. The water quality, monitored by the WWF, supports the riverside habitats where nectar-bearing plants grow.

06

Wild pollinators

Bumblebees, mason bees, Vanessa and Polyommatus butterflies. Our Apis mellifera ligustica share the land with dozens of wild species.

In numbers

A yearin the apiary

How far the bees usually forage
3 km

A bee normally gathers nectar within 2–3 km of the hive, and only goes further if there are no flowers nearby. Around the Priscilla apiary that circle covers almost 30 km² of Oasis and hills.

Of honey per hive in a good season
60+ kg

With a favourable spring a hive can produce 60–80 kg; in years of drought or late frost it drops to 20–30 kg.

Bees in a hive at the summer peak
50.000

A healthy hive in summer holds 50,000–80,000 bees. A worker lives 6–7 weeks.

Days of acacia bloom
8–12

Rain, wind or unusual heat can shrink the window to 5 days and wipe out the harvest.
